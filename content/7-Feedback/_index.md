## Overall Assessment

### 1. Work Environment  
**Rating: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (5/5)**

The work environment at FCAJ, especially the space on the 26th floor of Bitexco Tower, left a very strong impression on me.

- **Physical Space:** The office is modern, clean, well-lit, and comfortable enough to focus. The city view is a small bonus, but it makes long working hours more pleasant.
- **Team Atmosphere:** Everyone is friendly, open, and willing to help. When I ran into issues, I could always talk to the seniors without feeling awkward or pressured.
- **Facilities:** The internet is stable, and the desks, chairs, air conditioning, power outlets, and monitors for workshops/events are all fully prepared.
- **Location:** Being in the heart of District 1 makes commuting, eating out, or casual off-hour exchanges very convenient.

What I appreciate most is that the environment is both professional and not overly stressful. People work seriously but still maintain a relaxed, warm, and respectful vibe.

**Small suggestion:** FCAJ could organize more recurring team-building activities, such as monthly team lunches, internal mini-workshops, quarterly hackathons, or offsites. These would help members, especially interns, get to know each other better and strengthen team cohesion.

---

### 2. Mentor and Team Admin Support  
**Rating: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (5/5)**

Throughout my internship, I received excellent support from both my mentor and the team admin.

- **Mentor/Technical Lead:** The mentor didn't just show me how to do things — they also explained the reasoning behind each technical decision. During code reviews or architecture discussions, they often asked questions to guide me toward thinking and finding solutions on my own before revealing the answer. This approach helped me understand the essence of each problem instead of simply following steps.
- **Code Review:** The review sessions were constructive and highly educational. I received feedback not only on whether the code was correct, but also on security, performance, maintainability, and how to write cleaner code for others to read.
- **Team Admin:** Administrative matters such as onboarding, granting access, setting up AWS accounts, tracking billing, and providing equipment were all handled quickly. Thanks to that, I could focus more on the technical side.

What impressed me most was the mentor's consistent encouragement of the mindset: **“Try to figure it out first, but don’t hesitate to ask when you truly need help.”** This significantly improved my self-learning, debugging, and sense of ownership.

## Some Additional Questions

### What did you find most satisfying during the internship?

The most satisfying thing for me was experiencing a relatively complete project lifecycle — from initial architecture sketches and ideas to having CI/CD, monitoring, logging, technical documentation, and operational processes in place.

In addition, I really enjoyed the moments when I had to figure things out on my own. For example, when building a Python package from a Windows environment to run on AWS Lambda Linux, I spent quite a lot of time tracking down the root cause, and ultimately resolved it by building dependencies for the platform `manylinux2014_x86_64`. It was a small issue, but it gave me a much clearer understanding of the difference between a local environment and a production environment.

Another thing that boosted my confidence was that near the end of the internship, I was able to explain the EventBridge + SQS + DLQ pattern to newer interns and why that model should be used. When I could present it to others, I realized that I hadn't just “made it work” — I actually understood the problem quite deeply.

### What do you think the company could improve for future interns?

1. **A short AWS Bootcamp before starting the project**  
   For those with limited AWS experience, the first week can be quite challenging because they have to get familiar with the AWS Console, CLI, IAM, Lambda, API Gateway, CloudWatch, and the project workflow all at once. FCAJ could organize a short 3–5 day bootcamp on the foundational services and basic working processes to help interns get up to speed faster.

2. **A more flexible mechanism for Office Visit/attendance requirements**  
   During two consecutive months of internship, I proactively registered for more than 22 Office Visit days, not counting community events. However, because the number of spots is limited and requires approval, meeting the required attendance sometimes depended heavily on whether I could secure a slot.  

   I hope the company could consider a more flexible mechanism, such as:
   - Recognizing the number of registrations and the proactive spirit of participation;
   - Counting community events toward attendance;
   - Publishing the Office Visit schedule earlier;
   - Or adjusting the required number of sessions to match the actual number of available slots.

   This would be fairer for those who are eager to participate fully but unfortunately couldn’t get a spot.

3. **A Technical Writing Workshop**  
   A short session on writing Architecture Decision Records (ADRs), runbooks, postmortems, or handover documents would be very helpful. These are important skills in a real working environment, but students often don’t get thorough training on them at school.

### Would you recommend friends to intern here? Why?

**Yes, I would definitely recommend it, and I rate this experience 10/10.**

The reason is that the program doesn’t just assign interns simulation-style tasks — it gives them the opportunity to work on real technical problems: system design, data processing, security, monitoring, CI/CD, and operational optimization.

Here are the points I think FCAJ does well:

- **Real-world projects:** Interns get exposure to technologies and processes close to a production environment.
- **Mentors invested in teaching:** They don’t just assign tasks — they help interns understand how to think and make technical decisions.
- **Modern tech stack:** Serverless, Event-Driven, AI/ML, Cloud Security, and Data Engineering are all valuable directions for today’s career landscape.
- **Balance between autonomy and support:** Interns are encouraged to explore and experiment, but there is always someone ready to help when needed.
- **Great community:** Interns have access to community activities, technical events, and people already working in the AWS/Cloud field.

However, I think prospective applicants should prepare some basic knowledge in advance — such as Git, HTTP, JSON, Python or JavaScript, along with fundamental concepts of EC2, S3, Lambda, and IAM. For those who are completely new to cloud, the initial phase might be a bit intense.

## Suggestions and Future Intentions

### Suggestions to Improve the Internship Experience

1. **Capstone Presentation Day**  
   FCAJ could organize an official demo day where interns present their products, architecture, and lessons learned to leadership, mentors, or guests from the AWS community. This would be a great opportunity for interns to practice presentation skills, add something to their portfolio, and receive recognition for their hard work during the internship.

2. **Support for AWS Certification Fees**  
   If possible, FCAJ could cover part or all of the AWS Associate exam fee for interns who complete the program successfully. This would be a strong motivator for them to continue learning and growing after the internship.

3. **Build an Intern Alumni Network**  
   A LinkedIn, Slack, or Discord group for FCAJ Intern Alumni would help past interns stay connected, share job opportunities, exchange technical knowledge, and support one another in their career development.

4. **Open-Source Some Suitable Components**  
   For components that do not contain internal or sensitive information, FCAJ could consider open-sourcing certain utilities, templates, or demo architecture. This would contribute to the community while also giving interns public artifacts to include in their portfolios.
